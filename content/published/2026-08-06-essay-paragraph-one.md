An investor told a room of founders yesterday: write me an essay on how we make money together.

This is paragraph one. I'm writing it in public, before I know the ending.

He also said a VC's job is to spot champions. Whether I'm one gets tested, not claimed. But here's my internal bet. I joined Turing early and spent six years inside while it became exactly what VCs mean by champion. I watched management decisions get made. The org structure built, strained, rebuilt. The metrics designed, then redesigned when they bent behavior the wrong way. My bet: six years of watching a champion get built from the inside transfers.

Now the part I can already defend.

A recruiter working one role today opens up to 1,000 CVs. Reading that stack yields maybe 5 candidates per hour. Sourcing yields 3. Reading wins on volume, yet recruiters keep drifting toward sourcing. Because those 5 aren't selective. Inbound is keyword-matched noise, optimized against the very ranker that scored it.

That's the problem we took at StepUp. The reading and the ranking come off the recruiter's desk, and the inbound stack becomes selective again. Then we hand them an interview plan and a cheat sheet: a page that tells a non-technical recruiter what a strong answer sounds like, so they can hear the gap between someone who has shipped and someone who has read about shipping.

That part works. I've watched it work.

Now the most exciting part.

I'm holding a piece of value with maybe six knobs on it. Price. Segment. Which slice of the workflow I own. How far the automation goes. I turn one, watch the response, turn it back. Somewhere in that grid is the setting where the circuit closes and current runs through the whole thing.

So, how do we make money together? Not by me writing a convincing essay. By finding that setting while the market is still this noisy.

Paragraph two gets written when a knob clicks.
