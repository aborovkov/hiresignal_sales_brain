Most technical interviews are decided by the first answer. That is the whole problem.

You ask how they would design the thing. They answer well. Structured, confident, the right words in the right order. You write down strong hire.

Now ask the same person a second question about that same answer.

Not a harder question. A deeper one. Why that database and not the other one. What breaks first when traffic triples. What would you give up to make it simpler.

People who have actually built the thing get more specific. The explanation grows detail as you push, because there is something underneath it. Usually they volunteer what they got wrong the first time they shipped it.

People who prepared for the interview get vaguer. Same confidence, less content. They restate the first answer in new words. Some switch to a different architecture halfway and never notice they contradicted themselves.

That gap is the one thing in the loop that is hard to rehearse.

And most processes never open it. One question per topic, move on, next section, out of time. The scorecard rewards a polished first answer, which is exactly the artifact that preparation produces.

I have sat in debriefs where two interviewers defended opposite conclusions about the same candidate. Both had notes. Both were reasonable. Almost every time, the split traced back to one of them going a level deeper and the other running out of clock.

For a long time I thought the fix was a better question bank. It is not.

It is a rule. Never grade the first answer. Ask again on the same thing, twice, and grade how the answer changes.

Costs nothing to try in your next loop.

What is your second question?
