## The false positive moved upstream
- **Pillar:** Authority
- **Hook angle:** The most expensive false positive now happens *before* the interview — at the resume. A JD pasted into an LLM produces a near-perfect keyword match, so the match score rewards exactly the thing that's now automatable. Recruiters burn hours on candidates who look perfect on paper and collapse live.
- **Formula:** F10 Contrarian + Historical Receipts (or F4 Time-Anchor Confession)
- **Source:** senior staffing operator, unprompted, named this as the single feature that would make them look at a new hiring tool
- **Status:** idea

## Tool #91
- **Pillar:** Authority
- **Hook angle:** A new hiring tool no longer competes on whether it's good. It competes for a slot in a stack of 90 tools and 5 ATS. The bar isn't "is this better" — it's "does this survive integration friction." Most evaluation tools die in that gap, not on capability.
- **Formula:** F10 Contrarian + Historical Receipts
- **Source:** agency objection pattern — "we have 90 tools, we don't know how to incorporate yours"
- **Status:** idea

## Reps, not logos
- **Pillar:** Personal Narrative
- **Hook angle:** An evaluation system earns trust the same way an engineer does — reps under real conditions, not credentials on paper. The instinct to chase the enterprise logo first is backwards: signal systems have to be battle-tested on small, fast, low-red-tape teams before anyone senior will trust the number. (Tie back to hiring signal: trust in a score comes from calibration under real use, not from the size of the customer.)
- **Formula:** F3 Year-over-Year Pivot (or F8 Paid-vs-Free Reversal)
- **Source:** GTM advisor pushback on enterprise-first sequencing
- **Status:** idea
