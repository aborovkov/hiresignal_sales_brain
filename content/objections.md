# Objections

Living log of objections heard in outreach, deduped by meaning. Each entry keeps
the verbatim objection, who raised it and when, the answer used, the outcome,
and the current best known answer.

### "In Latvian market we barely have 100 applicants per months, so there is no need in automatisation"
- Who/stage: in-house HR recruiter (IT/Fintech), USPEX, Latvia, contacted stage (crm/violeta-jakubova.md)
- Date: 2026-07-30
- Answer used: conceded the volume angle was wrong for the market, pivoted to the polish-vs-depth gap in candidate answers, then offered the interview-questions product ("generates a list of opened questions you can ask from a candidate and it scores answers")
- Outcome: pending, thread alive
- Best known answer: for low-volume markets drop the CV-sorting pitch entirely and lead with interview quality: few candidates means every bad senior hire costs more, and the questions product needs no volume to be useful

### "I am sorry, but this AI response is very hard to read"
- Who/stage: in-house HR recruiter (IT/Fintech), USPEX, Latvia, replied stage, triggered by a long AI-styled recovery message (crm/violeta-jakubova.md)
- Date: 2026-07-30
- Answer used: honest admission, no defense: "sorry ... myself I am not a great connection establisher, indeed AI helped me:)"
- Outcome: defused, thread stayed alive
- Best known answer: own it in one short human line, then keep every following message 2-3 plain sentences max. Prevention beats recovery: recovery and objection-handling messages must be as short as first touches, not longer

### "У меня лично болит не в том, что мне проскорить, мне проскорить не болит"
- Who/stage: hands-on agency recruiter, contingency (%), Montenegro, meeting stage after a live demo (crm/nataly-lalova.md)
- Date: 2026-08-06
- Answer used: none - pivoted to building an email connector for her LinkedIn applications instead of defending the scoring layer
- Outcome: trial accepted, purchase declined, relationship reframed as referral
- Best known answer: concede the scoring layer immediately to any recruiter who already runs one in an LLM, then move to the boundary she named herself - volume. Small batches she handles fine; "когда ты приходишь до больших объемов, то все становится проблемой". Sell ingestion at volume and the cross-interview signal model, not the score

### "На что-то подписываться не готова, а вот матчить - да"

### "Это проблемы кандидата, потому что он мне все указал"

- Date: 2026-08-06

- Outcome: differentiator landed flat

### "due to security I will not open any links"
- Who/stage: freelance tech recruiter / HR & TA specialist, Tbilisi, contacted -> replied 23 minutes after the first DM (crm/tika-sharubanashvili.md)
- Date: 2026-08-06
- Answer used: "oh wow.. I didn't know that.. sorry" + "can I send you a couple of screenshots then?" + "when can I write you again?" - three messages in one pass, two questions, and an apology for something that was not our mistake
- Outcome: **RESOLVED same evening.** Screenshots were sent ~20 min later; she replied within 2 minutes with product questions (how video screening works, whether it is recorded) and the security topic never came back. She closed the evening with an unprompted verdict on the images - "Program looks like understandable" - so the screenshot pack did not merely unblock the thread, it carried the explanation on its own, with no call and no link. Not a rejection at any point - the offer survived, only the delivery mechanism was refused
- Best known answer: do not apologise, and do not argue for the link. Acknowledge in one line and move the proof to a format that never leaves LinkedIn - an image or PDF of a finished board sent as an attachment. Where geography allows, meet in person and open the board on your own laptop: it removes the objection completely instead of working around it. One message, one ask
- **Structural note, bigger than this contact:** every demo asset we currently lead with is a link to app.stepup.services, and we have no link-free version. Recruiters handling candidate PII are exactly the population most likely to have a no-unknown-links rule. Build the image/PDF board pack - now field-proven to unblock a stalled thread, so make it the DEFAULT proof asset rather than the fallback
- **Check first:** in this thread the link rendered malformed, with the following sentence absorbed into the anchor and a stray second hyperlink. Verify how the message actually looked when sent before treating this as policy rather than a broken message that simply looked like phishing

### "I almost never make mistakes because I'm an engineer myself"
- Who/stage: offshore team builder / global technical recruiter, Istanbul, replied -> closed same day (crm/elena-ustinova.md)
- Date: 2026-08-07
- Context: terminal form of the internal-owner deflection. Asked whether the client's interview load was a constraint, she did not answer the load question at all - she reframed to competence and asserted she does not make hiring mistakes because she is an engineer
- Answer used: none. "I see.. thanks for replying" and closed
- Outcome: `lost`, and correctly so. Whole cycle - invite accepted, first DM, objection, counter, close - took about 70 minutes
- Best known answer: **do not answer it.** There is no reply to "I am an engineer and I do not make mistakes" that is not either a challenge to their competence or a request that they doubt themselves. Both cost the connection and neither converts. Close warmly and leave the door open
- **Why it is worth logging anyway:** this objection is a qualification tool, not a wall. It only appears from people whose position contains no evaluation pain - they either do the technical read themselves or hand it entirely to someone who does. The load question ("has the interview load ever come up as a constraint?") surfaces it in one message. Ask it early of anyone who says someone else owns the technical read, and it sorts them immediately: a strained owner is a live deal, a confident one is a fast no
- Set `lost`, not `parked` - parking needs a nameable revisit trigger and there is none here
