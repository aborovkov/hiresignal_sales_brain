## Notes

### Our opener premise was wrong, and she said so politely
The message asserted that her technical read is the only one that exists. It is the opposite: the
client owns evaluation end to end. She did not treat it as a reason to disengage, but the angle
itself was a miss.

Lesson for the offshore / staff-supply cut: do not assume the middleman carries the technical read.
For this shape the buyer question is not "how do you evaluate" but "what does it cost you when the
client's engineers reject someone you sent." Ask that first, then position.

### The answer was the right shape - one habit undercut it
Validating the client's setup rather than arguing it is a bottleneck is exactly the best-known answer
recorded after the Fernando Eillman thread died from doing the opposite. That part was right.

The wedge in the second message is also correct and specific to her position: she is the supply side,
so her incentive is to never waste the client's engineer time and never look bad sending a weak
candidate. "Reject low performers before sending to your client" speaks to her margin and her
credibility, not to a screening feature.

What weakened it: **the first message ended on a genuine open question about interview load, and the
second message answered it for her before she could.** That question was the qualifying question of
the whole thread - whether the client's engineers are actually strained. Asking it and then filling
the silence with a pitch gives away the answer we needed. Ask, then stop.

This is the fourth thread today sent as two messages where one would do (Tika, Babilina, Madiha, now
Elena). It is becoming the house style by accident rather than by choice.

Also: the promise wording. "Reject low performers before sending" sits close to the line on the
approved three-tier screening form (obvious no removed, worth-a-conversation surfaced, borderline
calls stay with the recruiter). It does not overclaim, but the approved phrasing is safer with
someone whose client will hear it second-hand. And there is a stray backslash at the end of the
message.

### Next
Her answer on interview load decides the thread. If the client's engineers are strained, the
pre-filter sells itself. If they are not, she is a poor fit and should be parked rather than worked -
she has no evaluation pain of her own to solve.

## Close - 2026-08-07, same day. Disqualified on pain, not on fit.

### What actually happened
The qualifying question was "has the interview load ever come up as a constraint on their end." She
did not answer it. She answered a different question - whether mistakes happen at all - and the
answer was that they do not, because she is an engineer herself.

That is the end of the thread, and correctly so. Her position has no evaluation pain in it: the
client's engineers interview every candidate, and she screens with her own engineering judgment
before that. There is no cost for us to reduce. Twenty minutes of her time confirmed what two weeks
of follow-ups would have.

Set to `lost` rather than `parked` on purpose. Parking implies a revisit trigger, and there is none
we can name - she would have to start making placements she cannot personally vet, which is not a
change we can watch for.

### The close was handled right
"I see.. thanks for replying" and out. No argument, no "are you sure that scales", no attempt to
convert a clean no into a debate. Arguing with "I am an engineer and I do not make mistakes" is
unwinnable and insulting, and it would have cost a connection worth keeping. Two messages again, and
"Frieday" is a typo in the last thing she reads, but the judgment was correct.

### The useful part
This is the fastest full cycle in the pipeline so far: invite accepted, first DM, objection, answer,
counter, close - all inside about seventy minutes. The qualifying question did its job. It did not
produce a sale, it produced a fast, cheap, definitive no, which is the second best outcome and far
better than a polite maybe that eats follow-ups for a month.

Worth reusing: ask the load question early on any contact who says someone else owns the technical
read. The answer sorts them in one message.
