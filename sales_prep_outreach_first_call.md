Sales Prep, Outreach, and the First Call
What you need to do to start selling — 2025



Disclaimer: What follows doesn't always work in every market — local mentality and business culture change what lands.


What Are We Selling?
A sale is built on solving a problem. A few realities to keep in mind:

You're selling to several stakeholders at once.
You have a long deal cycle made up of multiple stages.
At each stage, some of your leads will drop off.


ICP — Why Do They Want This, and Who Needs It?
To understand how to sell, you have to know your buyer.

What pains do they have? How and when do they decide to buy? What do they do to find a solution they're willing to pay for?

Define your ICP across:

Industry
Company size
Location
Buyer job titles
Additional info
A Note on Stakeholders
Financial buyer — controls the budget.
Technical buyer — evaluates whether it works.
Influencer — shapes the decision without owning it.
Coach — your internal ally who guides you through the process.


USP — What It Is and Why You Need It
What is a USP, and how many do you need?

Strong product positioning is short and concrete. Examples of the genre:

All-in-one workspace to write, plan, and collaborate.
The easiest way to accept payments online.
Video communications that just work.
Design. Prototype. Collaborate. All in the browser.
Think and collaborate visually.
What Does Your Product Actually Do?
Boil it down to the value it delivers:

Saves time
Simplifies
Informs
Makes money

Once you understand your ICPs and have your USP assembled, keep moving.


Where Do You Go for Leads?
Recall that you need to understand how your ICP currently closes the problem when it comes up.
Where do they look for a solution today?
How do they make the decision to buy?
Based on that, choose the channel to reach your leads.

Possible channels:

LinkedIn
Email
Calls
Online magazines
Communities and recommendations


How Do You Compose the Message?
Combine ICP and USP. Create a message for each ICP.
Define the value you'll give them.
Identify the numbers they care about.
Add social proof.
Add a hook at the start of the message.
Never forget the call to action.


Building the Lead List
Use LinkedIn and BuiltWith.
Pull attendee lists from conferences.
Use Apollo, ZoomInfo, Datanyze.
If doing it yourself takes too long, hire someone on Upwork.
Reviewing Your Draft
You now have a lead list and a draft message. Reread it and grade it against these rules:

Show the value immediately in the outreach message.
Personalize it.
Keep the message short.
DON'T TALK ABOUT YOURSELF.
DON'T USE A LOT OF LINKS.
Be an understandable solution.


The Ideal Message Structure
(This is hard to pull off, but here's the target.)

Subject line: Spark interest or promise useful information.
Sentence 1: A piece of personal information tied to the lead.
Sentence 2: Build around their potential pains and risks.
Sentence 3: How do you solve those pains?
Sentence 4: Social proof.
Sentence 5: CTA.

Email approaches that work: lead with value, and personalize.


The First Call — "Now We'll Close It!"
You won't close it.
Why Do You Need the First Call?
To get to know your lead better — BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline).
To understand how best to present the product.
If there's time, to show the product.
How to Run It
Small talk.
Agenda.
Find out why the lead came to the call.
Learn as much as you can about them and their pains (this is where BANT lives).
Figure out which pains you can close.
Demo.
Pricing.
Next steps.
Stay emotional enough to keep the lead engaged.
Standard Mistakes
Monologuing.
Too much information about your company and yourself.
Not asking the lead questions.
Talking about features instead of value.
Not hitting the lead's personal pains.
No examples or use cases.
Reusing the same demo for every lead.
How to Prepare
Describe the potential problems of each ICP.
Describe the values that matter to them.
Describe the functionality that closes the problems they care about and delivers the value they want.
Prepare a list of questions to ask on any such call in advance.
Identify the "moments of excitement" and how to trigger them.
Lay out your pricing policy.


The Most Important Thing: The Moment of Excitement
A three-step move:

First, I want to show you "A", because "B".
You mentioned you have task "V", which comes out of "B". Do you think "A" solves your task?
What other problems do you think "A" + "A1" + "A2" could solve for you?

Example:

Let me show you how our platform can quickly create courses (A), because you mentioned you don't like spending time on that (B).
You mentioned you have a task to quickly launch high-quality internal training. Do you think the fast course creation I just showed you closes that task?
And what other problems do you think our platform could solve for you?
