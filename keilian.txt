Hey Keilian,

Quick follow-up from today’s call.

I synced with my team and mapped out how we evaluate AI-native engineers.

Our process focuses on whether candidates can use AI effectively while still operating as strong engineers independently.

1. Resume Validation (5–10 min)
We verify:

- employment consistency and tenure
- actual hands-on ownership vs. passive involvement
- credibility of stack claims

This helps filter resume inflation before spending engineering time.

2. Core Engineering Assessment (30–45 min)
Baseline competency across:

- languages and frameworks
- architecture and system design
- debugging and reasoning
- communication

Strong AI usage does not compensate for weak engineering fundamentals.

3. AI-Native Evaluation (15 min)
We evaluate real-world AI workflows:

- where AI accelerated delivery
- where AI failed
- validation and verification habits
- context management
- MCP/tooling usage
- permission and security awareness

The goal is to assess workflow maturity, not familiarity with trendy tools.

4. Practical Exercise (45–90 min)
Depending on the role, we use one of four formats:

- Existing Codebase Change
 Small legacy project with unclear requirements.
 We observe whether candidates investigate before prompting AI, validate assumptions, and protect existing behavior.

- Spec-First Task
 Candidate writes requirements and acceptance criteria before implementation.
 Tests clarity of thinking and ability to structure work independently.

- TDD with AI
 Candidate writes failing tests first, then implements with AI assistance.
 We evaluate test quality, validation discipline, and review rigor.

- Codebase Exploration
 Candidate navigates an unfamiliar domain using AI tools.
 We assess how they verify information, identify source-of-truth systems, and manage uncertainty.

5. Code Review Discussion (20 min)
Candidate walks through trade-offs, risks, and reasoning behind changes.

We specifically evaluate whether they treat AI-generated code as production code requiring critical review.

6. Documentation & Research (25 min)
We assess:

- specs
- ADRs
- PR descriptions
- research approach for unfamiliar domains

A strong signal is when documentation and reasoning appear -before implementation rather than after as justification.

7. Team Adoption & Leadership (10–15 min, senior+ only)
How would they introduce AI-assisted engineering practices across a larger team?

We evaluate:

- standards
- governance
- security
- review culture
- rollout strategy

Some of the red flags we consistently detect:

- resume inconsistencies
- “I let AI do everything”
- inability to explain validation steps
- accepting generated diffs without review
- weak testing discipline
- shipping without security awareness

We deliver full interview artifacts:
- interview recording
- transcription
- structured evaluation notes
- AI-assisted analysis with reasoning behind fit/no-fit recommendations

Happy to run a pilot with one of your clients if you'd like to see the process in practice.

- Alexey

I understand the appeal of automation - we pursued it heavily at Turing. But I should warn you: it's trickier than it looks.

Automated assessments do expand your funnel, but they also introduce significant noise. Without extremely robust proctoring, you end up with high false positive rates. 

Candidates are sophisticated about gaming these systems, and we found that even advanced anti-cheating measures - including ML models analyzing keystroke patterns - couldn't reliably catch it.

After years of experimentation, we learned that human-conducted interviews remain the gold standard for quality. 

A trained interviewer on a live call catches things automation simply can't. The real cost optimization isn't removing humans entirely - it's having the right humans. 

Train recruiters to conduct structured technical screens instead of pulling senior engineers offline. You get the same evaluation quality at a fraction of the cost.
