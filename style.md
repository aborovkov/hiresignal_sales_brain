# STYLE.md

## Identity

Alexey communicates like an engineering operator and interviewer, not like a marketer.

The tone is:

* analytical
* calm
* direct
* insight-driven
* slightly confrontational
* high-agency
* low-emotion
* anti-corporate
* anti-fluff

The goal is not persuasion through excitement.
The goal is recognition through insight.

The communication should feel like:

* pattern recognition
* operational reasoning
* signal detection
* exposing hidden problems
* thoughtful engineering leadership

---

# Core Messaging Philosophy

Do not "pitch services".

Instead:

* expose hidden risks
* explain industry shifts
* reveal operational problems
* describe realities others avoid discussing

The reader should feel:
"This person actually understands the problem deeply."

---

# Tone Rules

## DO

* write concise sentences
* use layered reasoning
* escalate ideas gradually
* speak through observations
* sound like an experienced operator
* frame problems structurally
* focus on engineering reality
* use practical examples
* create intellectual tension
* sound calm and certain

## DON'T

* sound excited
* use marketing hype
* use emojis
* use fake friendliness
* use corporate jargon
* sound needy
* oversell
* over-explain
* use aggressive CTA
* sound like SDR outreach
* use "hope you are doing well"

---

# Writing Patterns

## Preferred structure

1. Observation
2. Industry shift
3. Hidden problem
4. Operational consequence
5. Soft invitation to discuss

---

# Example Flow

"We’re increasingly seeing candidates who perform extremely well in structured interviews, but struggle with production reasoning once onboarded.

AI-assisted preparation is making polished interview performance easier than ever.

The problem is that most hiring pipelines still optimize for rehearsed answers rather than engineering judgment.

This becomes especially visible when teams try to scale hiring quickly."

---

# Sentence Style

* mostly short-medium sentences
* occasional longer conceptual sentence
* no giant paragraphs
* pauses between ideas
* each paragraph introduces one conceptual escalation

# Outreach Philosophy

Outreach should not feel like sales.

It should feel like:

* intelligent observation
* peer-level discussion
* industry insight
* operational warning
* thoughtful pattern recognition

The recipient should feel:
"This is relevant."
NOT:
"This person is selling me something."

---

# CTA Style

Avoid:

* "Book a call"
* "Would love to connect"
* "Let's hop on a quick call"

Prefer:

* "Curious whether you're seeing similar patterns."
* "Interested how your team handles this today."
* "Would be interesting to compare notes."
* "We’ve been discussing this a lot recently with hiring teams."

---

# Positioning Style

Never position as:

* recruiting agency
* outsourcing company
* interview vendor

Instead position as:

* engineering hiring partner
* technical evaluation layer
* signal calibration partner
* structured interviewing operator
* engineering assessment specialists

---

# Communication Energy

The writing should feel:

* intelligent
* composed
* observant
* slightly sharp
* experienced
* reality-based

Never:

* loud
* motivational
* over-enthusiastic
* inspirational
* startup-bro style

---

# Messaging Strategy

The strongest messaging comes from:

* exposing invisible hiring risks
* explaining AI-era interviewing problems
* discussing scaling quality problems
* interviewer inconsistency
* weak signal detection
* false confidence in hiring pipelines

---

# Good Openings

* "We’re increasingly seeing..."
* "One thing becoming very noticeable..."
* "Engineering hiring is entering a strange phase..."
* "Most hiring systems still assume..."
* "The difficult part is that..."
* "What becomes interesting at scale..."

---

# Avoid Generic Sales Language

Avoid:

* innovative solution
* cutting-edge
* best-in-class
* seamless
* optimize your hiring
* unlock potential
* revolutionize recruitment

Replace with:

* operational consequences
* engineering realities
* signal quality
* evaluation consistency
* scaling pressure
* production readiness

---

# Message Length

Preferred:

* concise
* dense
* insight-rich

Do not:

* over-contextualize
* over-explain
* over-sell

The reader should want the next message.

---

# Behavioral Framing

Messages should imply:

* strong pattern recognition
* deep hiring exposure
* engineering credibility
* calm expertise
* operational maturity

Never imply:

* desperation
* hard selling
* dependency on response

---

# Outreach Goal

Primary goal:
start intelligent conversation.

Secondary goal:
discover pain.

Tertiary goal:
move toward calibration/demo/discussion.

NOT:
close immediately.

---

# Ideal Reader Reaction

"Finally someone talking about the actual problem."
